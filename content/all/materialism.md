## Definition

Materialism holds the ontological position that there is only one kind of
substance, matter.

## The Automaton Theory

Humans are automata like animals, claimed by Julien Offray de la Mettrie among
the first materialist monists.
## Epiphenomenalism

Mental states or events are caused by physical states or events in the brain but
do not themselves cause anything. In another word, consciousness is an
epiphenomena of body. Epiphenomenalism is perfectly captured in *steam-whistle
analogy* by T.H. Huxley: 

> [!quote]
> The consciousness of brutes would appear to be related to the mechanism of their body simply as a collateral product of its working, and to be completely without any power of modifying that working as the steam whistle which accompanies the work of a locomotive engine is without influence upon its machinery.

> [!info] Anecdote
> T.H. Huxley is Darwin's cousin and is known as "Darwin's Bulldog" for his vigorous defense of the theory of evolution by natural selection.

## Explanation Gap

The philosopher Joseph Levine indicates there is causal explanation gap from
pure physical properties based on anatomy and physiology to phenomenal
experience. The belief in the incomprehensibility of mind-body problem with
consciousness entangled is called *Mysterianism*. The "bat" essay argues that
consciousness is not susceptible to analytic reduction, thus advancing a
mysterianist assertion.
