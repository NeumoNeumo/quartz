# Where is your time

**Time black hole**: The event during which it provides with related attractive
information distracting one from the event itself.

Example
- Searching information on the Internet
- Watching open courses on bilibili.
- Pulling a rope but eventually dragging out a elephant is frequently used as a
classical metaphor for the time black hole.

Mechanism: Relevant labor-saving things always take precedence of noteworthy
but time-consuming things in our mind. 

**Active and passive time usage**: Using time actively means anticipating the
outcome before getting started and pursuing the anticipation afterwards and vice
versa. Time black hole is the set of time used passively.

> [!note] Experience
> Freshmen are often given plenty of trivia among which buries *bombs*, those
really important thing as examination from others. If you cannot figure the bombs
out, you are prone to be overwhelmed by meaningless things and regarded as a
handyman bustling around without aims. ^freshmen

# Organize your tasks

1. [[four_quadrants]]
2. closet organization method: 
	1. Throw encountered tasks into a *collection basket*: When encountering new tasks, do not make it interrupt you current task and lean into it immediately. Instead, throw it to a *collection basket*. 
	2. Categorize the tasks in the basket: Different task should be dealt with in different ways. For example, some tasks cannot be done right now because of the lack of some condition. Those tasks might be move to the *future list*. Similarly, other tasks may be moved to *cooperative projects*, *easy tasks*, e.t.c. 
	3. Post-process: For tasks that do not need further investigation like *easy tasks*, just do it. However, some complicated projects require a detailed plan as a post-procession.
	4. take actions

Note that these aforementioned tasks help you focus more on your current work
and make you good at disposing of them. However, you also need to examine
yourself from a higher level. So, make yearly, monthly and weekly plans.

# Finish tasks

1. Start from the **most important and possibly most difficult part**. The reason is as follows:
	1. From the perspective of the event itself. According to Pareto's principle, 80% value of a work lies in 20% of the most important part which is likely to be the most difficult part meanwhile.
	2. From the manager's perspective, the most important part is actually where assessment is instead of those nonsense trivialities. If you can hardly balance important things with trivialities, do the important one. A wise manager tends to give those trivialities to other people or allocates an assistant to you.
 2. **Pomodoro technique** is not only a method for concentration -- you may find the focused work time is too short for burdensome work -- but the break time frees yourself from tactical minutiae and think outside the box as well which is especially crucial for creative work. In another word, during the break time, you are still working but in another view.
 3. Embrace changes and exploit inertia. People don't like changes. But you have to embrace changes so as not to spend too much time on one thing for the diminishing marginal utility. Meantime, if you find it hard to begin a hard job, start from a rather easier part of it and the inertia leads you to continue the hard job.
