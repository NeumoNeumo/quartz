Four quadrants of time management matrix is proposed by Stephen Covey:

```text
          Important                  
             ▲                       
             │                       
  Schedule   │  Do First             
 e.g. PhD App│ e.g. Theory           
      TOEFL  │                       
─────────────┼──────────────► Urgent 
  Delete     │  Delegate             
 e.g. Zhihu  │ e.g. Engineer         
      BiliBil│                       
             │                       
```


## Criterion
Importance: Does it help you better achieve your dream? Is it really [[four_quadrants#Value|essential]]?
Urgency: Is it time-limited?
### Value
| Better              | Worse               |
| ------------------- | ------------------- |
| Systematic Learning | Fragmented Learning |
| Textbooks           | Paper               |
| Outstanding Papers  | Ordinary Papers     |
| Do a lot            | Learn a lot         |
| Writing             | Reading             |
## Attitude towards different quadrants

1. You should investigate most of your time in the second quadrant. Most events
in the first quadrant is what you have not disposed of in the second quadrant.
First quadrant is a horrendous disaster. The less you enter it, the better life
you'd have. The core of plans in the second quadrant is eliminating the three
enemies against time controlling: **procrastination, indecisiveness and
ambiguity of objects**. [[OKR]]-like methods can be employed. When making a
plan, you should always keep in mind which part is the key point of the task. As
shown in Pareto principle, solving the key problems is half the battle. Or in
Chinese, "抓主要矛盾，和矛盾的主要方面". And when you take action, starting from the key
problem is the most efficient. Sometimes the key problem is really hard.
Starting from a hard problem requires courage. So you may begin from an easy
action, for instance, searching for a paper. In short, you ought to start from
the key task while making an easy start for the key task. Remember the famous
quote of Franklin D. Roosevelt in his 1933 inaugural address, "**The only thing
we have to fear is fear itself**". ^three-enemies

2. If a leader is always busier than subordinates, think about if the leader
undertakes too much work in the fourth quadrant that could have been delegated.
Bill Oncken calls work in the fourth quadrant *monkeys on the back*. When you
take on others' responsibility, the monkey on their backs jumps to yours. Let
those monkeys back to where they should go. In the context of team management,
clarify delegation and responsibility.
