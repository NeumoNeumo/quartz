The points that I have already followed or have researvations about are not listed. Some pieces of advice are modified from my personal perspective. You may prefer reading these advices yourself.

Advice that is valuable to me:
1. What makes a good lecture
    1. Running overtime means you think your lecture is more important than others' schedule.
    2. Identify one audience whose work you are familar with. Adopt your content to reference their contribution. This ensures you have at least one attentive listener.
    3. Create memoriable impressions. An arbitrary conclusion, a slogen, or a joke. Don't leave your audience nothing after your lecture. If it does, what's the difference between your lecture and corporate jargon? Why do you waste your time and your audiences' on this lecture?
2. Writing books rather than papers usually contributes more substantially to the academic community. [[Advice_from_Hamming|Richard Hamming]] holds the same idea.
3. Every mathematician has only a few tricks. Most of the time, as in math competitions, learning the skills of your predecessors is enough to make you a winner. For more information about how math as a culture evolves, check [this uploader](https://space.bilibili.com/180947374)
4. Keep a dozen of your favorite problems in mind. Test against them whenever you learn a new result or technique.
