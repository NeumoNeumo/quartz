People are inherently shortsighted -- they often sacrifice massive long-term gains for trivial short-term rewards. As a result, many conventional strategies fail because they either impose long-term penalties or promise distant benefits -- yet humans just don't care about future.

To counter this, we must tie rewards or punishments to the *present moment*. For example:
- Daily streak: The longer you maintain a streak, the more time and effort you've investigated in preserving it. Every successful task in the streak represents a genuine "proof of work" and sunk cost.
- **Chained Pomodoro System**: If you failed to complete a pomodoro, the chain breaks, and you need to start over.

Now let's address the *distractions* during a Pomodoro session. The broken window effect poses a major threat: one small lapse can snowball, drastically reducing your focus. To combat this, we enforce the **Always Again Rule**. If an action is allowed in this Pomodoro, it's permanently allowed. This forces you to consider the future consequences of your choices right now.

Lastly, sometimes, we are afraid to start a pomodoro because we are afraid of breaking the chain. Then it comes to the "**reserving chain**". It is hard to start at once, but it is easier to accept starting in 15 minutes. So we can make aother chain used to deal with things like reserve a main pomodoro. The Always Again rule also applies to this chain.

From a higher perspective, every major failure -- whether procrastination or distraction -- stems from a tiny, early-stage misstep where a small intervention could have made all the difference. That's where we should focus.

For example, You can’t wake up early? It’s probably because you were exposed to blue light from your phone the night before. The best fix isn’t buying a fancy alarm clock—it’s leaving your phone out of reach before bed. Repeat this enough, and it becomes automatic. Soon, you’ll wake up early without effort.

> "The skilled commander wins by fighting only the battles they’re sure to win." 善战者，胜于易胜者也。《孙子兵法》

Therefore, we can design a **habit tree**.
- Each node represents a habit.
- If you break one, the node—and all its dependent habits—are deleted.
As a consequence, the strongest roots are the simplest habits: effortless actions with outsized impact.
