# Sleep

## Biological Mechanism of Sleep

1. *Adenosine*(腺(嘌呤核)苷) builds up the longer we stay wake, which increases sleep pressure. For instance, caffeine in [[dopamine#Coffee|coffee]] acts as an adenosine antagonist which binds to the adenosine receptor.
2. *A circadian rhythm or circadian cycle* denotes the endogenous process responding to environmental factors every ~24 hours. For instance, after pulling an all-nighter and the adenosine levels are high, when the morning sunlight appears, the circadian rhythm can temporarily increase alertness.
	1. Wake up: cortisol from adrenal glands and adrenaline from adrenals and your brain released in response to the alarm clock or natural circadian cycle
	2. Sleepy: melatonin produced from your pineal gland released after the wakefulness signal by 12-14 hours controlled by cellular timers.

> [!caution]- Do NOT take melatonin except in rare cases
> 1. Melatonin affects other hormones especially for kids. Melatonin suppresses the onset of puberty. In kids, melatonin is released tonically through out the day instead of just after 12-14 hours of wakefulness. 
> 2. Melatonin helps you to fall asleep but not stay asleep. You may wake up after 3 hours of sleep and no longer fall asleep.
> 3. The listed dosage may significantly deviate from the actual values, which could differ by magnitudes as high as hundreds-fold according to *Why We Sleep* by Matthew Walker.

## Methods to Regulate Circadian Rhythm

### Light

1. Melanopsin-containing retinal ganglion cells (mRGCs, a type of neurons) perceive yellow and blue light when the sun is low in the sky and communicate that to the clock in your suprachiasmatic nucleus(视交叉上核) which has connections with every cell and organ in your body. Viewing sunlight through glass attenuates the activation. Higher angles of sunlight shift the spectrum, undercutting the effect as well. 10,000-50,000 lux (2-10 min) of light is sufficient to set the circadian clock. The light from smart phone is densely bright but lacks the amount of light. Additionally, watching sunset or circa sunset helps prevent melatonin release from the influence of other light that evening.
2. There's a diabolical feature that the longer you are awake the more sensitive you are to light and it's easier to trigger the activation of the clock, making you want to stay up later.
3. Light between 11 p.m. to and 4 a.m. suppresses the release of dopamine because it activates habenula(松果体缰), a.k.a. disappointment nucleus.
4. The position of light also matters. Those neurons that signal the central cycle mostly reside in the bottom half of your retina, viewing the upper visual field. Therefore, placing light low in the environment(e.g. candlelight, fireplace and campfires) is better for sleep, instead of overhead light. But moonlight is OK.
5. Light exposure when your eyelids are closed before you wake up also helps shifting the circadian clock earlier because some light can pass through eyelids.
6. Changing the spectral composition of self-luminous displays without changing their brightness settings may be insufficient for preventing impacts on melatonin suppression.[^2] But you can both lower the brightness and enable the night mode.
7. The contrast between the lightness after waking up for one or two hours and that before you go to bed for one or two hours is critical to circadian cycles. So experience bright light in the morning.

### Drugs & Supplements

1. Almost every drug has some effect on your circadian cycle.
2. Some forms of magnesium like threonate magnesium enhance sleep quality by increasing neurotransmitters like GABA, turning off the DPO.
3. Theanine and apigenin also help promote sleepiness. However, apigenin makes dreams unusually vivid, which may be problematic for sleepwalkers and it's also a potent estrogen inhibitor which should be avoided by women who want to remain some level of estrogen.
### Others

1. The intergeniculate leaflet regulates your circadian cycle through non-photic
influences including exercise and feeding. Therefore, exercise after getting up
also helps you become an earlier riser.

2. Heavier blankets significantly reduce the severity of insomnia.[^5][^6]

## Post-meal drowsiness

Both videos[^3][^4] discuss the mechanism underlying postprandial drowsiness. Free amino acids in the bloodstream cross the blood–brain barrier via the large neutral amino acid transporter LAT1. Under basal conditions, tryptophan competes with other large neutral amino acids for transport. Following a meal, insulin stimulates the uptake of glucose and most competing amino acids into peripheral tissues, increasing the relative availability of tryptophan in circulation. Consequently, tryptophan gains a competitive advantage at LAT1, facilitating its transport into the brain. Because tryptophan serves as a precursor for serotonin and melatonin synthesis, this mechanism may contribute to postprandial sleepiness. In addition, a shift from sympathetic to parasympathetic autonomic activity during digestion may play a secondary role.

Mitigation strategies:
- Reduce the amount of starchy carbohydrates in a meal.
- Consume foods in the following order: vegetables, protein and fats, carbohydrates.
- Engage in light physical activity after meals, such as walking.
