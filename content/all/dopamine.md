# Controlling Your Dopamine For Motivation, Focus & Satisfaction

## Properties of dopamine release

Tonic and phasic dopamine release: It describes the phenomena implicated in
learning, craving and motor activities, tonic being the low level baseline where
dopamine always circulates and phasic being the the spikes above the baseline.
The interaction between these two things regulates the release of dopamine.

Peaks from distinct activities take synergic effects, resulting in a higher
peak. **Counter-intuitively, the baseline after a peak would fall into a decline
proportional to the height of the peak below the initial value and eventually
raises back again.** This may have something to do with postpartum depression
and the mechanism of addiction.

## Neurobiological mechanism

### Neural circuits pertaining to dopamine

1. mesocorticolimbic pathway: ventral(bottom) tegmentum(floor) to ventral
striatum and prefrontal cortex. This is the classical reward pathway in all
mammals whereby dopamine influences motivation. Drugs like cocaine tap into this
pathway and disrupt it.
2. nigrostriatal pathway: substantia nigra(because the cells there are deeply
pigmented) to dorsal striatum. This is mainly for movement.

###  Communication between neurons and dopamine

Dopamine functions both as neurotransmitter (local synaptic release) and
neuromodulator (large-scale volumetric release). Dopamine is received by G
protein-coupled receptors(GPCT). So it take a while for dopamine to take effect.

After a peak, the readily releasable pool of dopamine is depleted, thus leading
to a lack of dopamine afterwards and psychological depression.

### Relations with motivation

The quality of your life and levels of your drive depend on the absolute value
of dopamine as well as the relative value of the current compared to your recent
experience, that is the ratio of the peak and the baseline.

For example, Drugs and supplements that increases dopamine both increase
synaptic release and volumetric release which means they increase the baseline
and the peak at the same time. You would not achieve more pleasure in the long
term in this way.

### Co-release

1. Dopamine has effects that are both excitatory and inhibitory. But it is
usually co-released with glutamate, a excitatory neurotransmitter. So dopamine
tends to stimulate sympathetical(交感神经的) arousal which means it increase our
levels of alertness.

2. Epinephrine(adrenaline) are actually manufactured from dopamine. So the peak
of dopamine often means the peak of epinephrine as well.

> [!note]- Difference between epinephrine and adrenaline
> They are the same molecule. But adrenaline is secreted from the adrenal glands
above the kidney while epinephrine is from the brain. Most of the time they can
be used interchangeably. 

## Dopamine in daily activities

### Short-term stimulation

Peak:
- Chocolate - 1.5x
- Sex - 2x (act and pursuit of it)
- Nicotine - 2.5x
- Cocaine - 2.5x
- Amphetamine - 10x
- Exercise - up to 2x (the more you enjoy the more dopamine released)

###  Immersion into cold water

The dopamine release slowly increases to 2.5x and sustains for ~3 hours. What
method you choose to resist the discomfort resulting from coldness, whether
distracting yourself from the chill, relaxing yourself or ramping up your level
of internal, has no effect for sake of dopamine release. The adaptation to a
previously supposed cold temperature subsides the dopamine peak.

For more information, check this [paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s004210050065)

### Coffee
Coffee is an exception in that it does not affect the dopamine release directly
but by upregulating D2, D3 receptors that receive dopamine. However, a number of
energy drinks and pre-workouts contain precursors of dopamine.

Some people do dopamine-evoking activities simultaneously to gain a higher peak.
However, this approach may create severe issues with motivation and energy right
after those experience, even a couple of days later according to the
aforementioned [[dopamine#Properties of dopamine release]]. ^layer-too-much

## Dopamine Schedules

### Three lifestyles

1. Alice is addicted to mobile games. It's apparent how the baseline of dopamine
would be lowered, eventually causing depression.
2. Bob really loves working out during which he communicates with his friends,
listening to music and podcast, drinks his favorite beverage. However, later, he
may does not feel as enthusiastic about exercising as before because he achieves
significantly high levels of dopamine release by layering too many pursuits too
many time. [[#^layer-too-much]]
3. Charlie takes all kinds of activities that he enjoys 7 days a week. There's
still a problem if we remember the tonic and phasic dopamine release property.
The dopamine baseline drops at an imperceptibly low rate over years. Finally,
Bob feels he cannot get pleasure from any activities.

### The optical way

The key lies in the random intermittent release of dopamine. (The same applies
to the strategy of casino, social media, video games and 爽文). Never expect to
achieve high levels of dopamine release every time engaged in activities. To use
the schedule in our daily life, a tool is **flipping a coin before we engage in
the activities to decide whether dopamine supportive elements like a coffee or
listening to music is allowed**. It's like designing your lifestyle as designing a game.

### Caveats

Working hard for sake of a reward that come afterward can make the work more
challenging and make us less likely to lean into hard work in the future. You
have to **enjoy the hard work itself**. Sometimes the work is really painful in
the short term. However, the mesocorticolimbic pathway is associated with the
forebrain. So you can **tell yourself the effort part is the good part**. It's a
lie based on the fact that you WANT it to feel better. So do not spike dopamine
before or after engaging in effort. Learn to spike from the effort itself.
(Intermittent fasting has the same mechanism to some extent that people get
dopamine release from deprivation instead of food because they think it good)

> [!example] 
Stanford researchers gave rewards for drawing to kids who liked drawing
intrinsically in nursery schools and kindergartens. Those kids had a much lower
tendency to draw on their own when researchers stopped giving rewards.

>[!example]
Those who imagine a hardworking person as their future are more successful than
those who imagine an already successful person as their future. Perhaps that's
because the former pays more attention to the hark work itself while the latter
focus more on the result.
