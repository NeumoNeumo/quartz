[You and Your Research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw) by Hamming.

Matthew's effect is pervasive in society. You have to prove yourself having the ability to do research before having the leeway to only do research without the trifles of teaching and engineering. So it is necessary to do something outstanding. The more ordinary a work is, the more surplus value is exploited.

You only live once. Life is like an MC server--leave something worthwhile to the world.

It is not "fame is a matter of luck" but "luck favors the prepared mind". If you have not gone all out for it, why should you blame the disregard of Fortuna. Most accomplished people talk shop all the time.

Do important work(Hamming spent 10% of his time thinking about what the important job is.) and talk with important people(or likely important). "If what you are doing is not important, not likely important, why are you doing it?" asked Hamming. You cannot always tackle important questions head-on; some are intractable due to limitations of the times. Many Nobel laureates no longer produce valuable work after they are awarded because they only want to to ultimate work. As a result, they couldn't do anything But what endowed them the prize is that they made some contribution. You have to plant the acorns which will grow into might oak trees.

Important works is sometimes hard. So don't expect to tackle them in a single swoop. Great scientist usually have 10-20 question in their mind. Whenever they get a clue how to attack, they rush to that problem and finish it off first.

The tolerance to ambiguity is vital for a researcher. They both believe and disbelieve.

**Overconfidence is better than lack of confidence.** If you don't think you can do good work, it's not likely that you are ever going to do it. "I ain't scared of nothing", said Shannon.

Whatever you do, you are going to do well. Whenever you do, you have a clear goal. Random walk leads to a distance proportional to the square root of N while walking directly with a clear vision leads to that proportional to N.

You are not first-class genius. You have to work harder. So why are you wasting your time reading all that non-sense stuff? **It is not what you do but how you do it.**

If you want to make progress, you have to change. Change does not guarantee progress but progress requires change.

In the early stages of a research career, the lack of sufficient research freedom and support is common due to the Matthew Effect. He faced similar challenges in his own early career. However, what truly matters is the ability to produce high-quality reearch even under unfavorable conditions. If you want to have more freedom, you need to make your advisor believe: "The more I leave him alone, the more he'd focus on his research--and the more he's gonna produce something valuable beyond my current topic".

Being successful is hard. Do you think it's worth it? It depends on your answer to the following question, "when you are in your old age, do you want to say I had a good time in life or I did something important, at least something I thought was important."
