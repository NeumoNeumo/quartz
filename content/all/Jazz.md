## Scales

The blues scale is essentially the Chinese pentatonic scale with an added blue note. It is merely one among dozens of scales used in jazz. In a single piece, different chords may employ different scales, which is one reason why newcomers often perceive jazz as dissonant.

## Rhythm

Swing rhythm: divides one beat into triplets, with the first note occupying the first two-thirds and the second note the remaining third. It is typically played on the hi-hat. In a broader sense, swing can also refer to the alternation of long and short beats, not necessarily in a strict 2:1 ratio.

Polyrhythm: for example, superimposing a quadruple subdivision in the drum set over a swing rhythm pattern.

Backbeat accent: for instance, in a quadruple subdivision of one beat, pop music typically follows a strong-weak-medium-weak pattern, whereas jazz often emphasizes a weak-strong-weak-strong pattern.

## Timbre

Jazz tends to be relatively traditional and conservative in its choice of instruments, such as piano, saxophone, trumpet, trombone, guitar, bass, and drum kit. This instrumental palette is a significant source of the distinctive "jazz sound."
