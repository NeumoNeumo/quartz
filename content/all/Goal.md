# Physiology

1. Amygdala(杏仁核) involves in the circuit associated with alertness, fear and
anxiety.

2. basal ganglia(基底节：指位于脑部的一组神经核团，包括纹状体、黑质和丘脑下核) has go pathway to generate
actions and no-go pathway to withhold or cease actions.

3. lateral prefrontal cortex(侧前额叶皮层) involves in the immediate and long-term
planning

4. orbitofrontal cortex(眶额叶皮层) evaluates current emotional and arousal states

# Strategy
## Beneficial Protocols
1. Use **verbs** to describe your goal

2. Define the deadline of a goal and how much time you want to spend on pursuing
your goal each day or each week. **Quarterly goal setting is critical**. 

3. Define a measurable metric to evaluate your progress and outcome.

4. Imaging the **positive outcome and achievement of your goal** before
initiating that day's work towards that goal gives you more energy. If you are
not so motivated, image the **failure and severe consequences** as
self-flagellation.

5. If you feel distracted in your goal-pursue block(30 min, 2 hours or so),
**pick a visual target** approximated the same distance that you'd be performing
your work and then **focus your eyes on that location** for at least 60 seconds.
Narrowing visual perception has an impact on your cognitive focus and
transiently increase your systolic blood pressure(收缩压).

6. Either consistently rewarding yourself during goal pursuit or regarding the
accomplishment of the goal as the only reward undermine your probability of
success. That why we need [[dopamine#The optical way|random intermittent reinforcement]]. The reward consists of physical rewards(like bangumi, movie) and mental rewards(think about the meaning of what you've done. Pat yourself on the back)

7. People tends to be more motivated at the beginning and end of a training
bout. That's where the *middle problem* arises. Breaking down the middle session
into multiple smaller sessions could help you focus more. For example, you feel
distracted on Wednesday, then breaking 2-hour goal pursuit session into 4
15-minute sessions and using some protocols mentioned between sessions to focus
on your goal.

>[!note] 
>Specificity and measurability is paramount.

## Common Myths

1. Post-it fallacy: Write your goal on post-it notes is useless because your
visual system adapts to whatever is regular in your environment. If you want to
make it work, put a new sticky on a new place every day.
 ^post-it-fallacy
2. The encourage in the early stage activates reward systems that then quickly
dissipate, which diminishes the possibility of achieving that goal. That's why
announcing your determination to the public before taking actions and receiving
positive incentive on the contrary may impede your goal pursuit. But you can
tell your goal to those who supervise you but do not encourage you too much.
That's where the meaning of catfish groups is: a immersive group of racing and
playing.
